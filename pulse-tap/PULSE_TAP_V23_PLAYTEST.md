## Local-choice method

Absolute v2.2 salience and its components are unchanged. Each beat now exposes previous/next comparisons, rank over ±2 source beats, local peak and component advantages. The preference score adds relative salience and rank. Dynamic programming preserves local four-beat quotas, protected tutorial/Landing choices and trailing burst limits, while reducing displacement to a deterministic tie-break. A final bounded local exchange replaces meaningfully weaker choices when safely interchangeable. Every exchange strictly increases total absolute salience, so the finite search terminates.

An inversion means at least 0.025 absolute salience loss to a skipped legal beat within ±2 indices in the same stage and four-beat window, replaceable without changing quota, violating ending/release, creating an interval under 180 ms, or exceeding the burst cap. Tutorial and Landing are excluded. This is a deliberately narrow, reproducible on-grid diagnostic. Zero measured inversions does not establish the subjectively best musical choice, nor exclude multi-beat exchanges or a stronger candidate outside that neighborhood. The before/after report distinguishes actual old weaker-to-stronger replacements from inversions resolved through changed density or constraints.

`PULSE_TAP_V23_COMPARISON.json` contains BOTH tracks' selected/skipped neighborhoods, component weaknesses, exact old/new index sets, selected/skipped means, rank, inversion evidence, high-salience misses, weak retained choices, continuity corrections and all-stage distributions. Travel uses a 375×667 CSS-pixel portrait field. The metrics include interval mean/median/min, recovery distribution and frequency, travel distribution, run histograms and base/unbroken-combo scoring opportunities. They do not prove subjective difficulty.
